# Keynote Devices — Palette & Spines

Primary reference for **Keynote mode**. These techniques were distilled from a
study of image-led keynote decks. They are **content-agnostic** — they depend on
properties of the *argument*, not the subject matter, so they transfer to a SaaS
pitch, a research readout, or a personal talk.

## Four framing rules (read before using the palette)

1. **Palette, not checklist.** Select only what the content affords. A strong deck uses maybe **4 of 16** devices. Using all sixteen is pastiche.
2. **Affordance triggers.** Each device below carries an *affords-when* condition tied to a content property. Match devices to what the source actually contains — a shocking number invites the giant-number device; a paradox invites the two-beat pair; a hinge that recontextualizes invites the reveal.
3. **Anti-pastiche rule.** Never manufacture what the source lacks. No invented coined words for content with no new concept, no false dread, no fabricated authority quotes. Devices serve the argument; drama the content doesn't hold reads as parody.
4. **Universal grammar vs. signature flavor.** The *structural grammar* — image-led slides, the caption box, beat titles, the spine menu, the base layouts — is **on by default** and fits any deck. The *signature tics* — coined vocabulary (#12), borrowed-authority quote stacks (#13), fear-of-the-future framing — are **opt-in flavor, off by default**; use them only when the source and audience clearly warrant it. Dry or technical input that affords few devices still renders cleanly on the base layouts (full-bleed + caption, wordless, motif) or leans hybrid toward Boardroom.

## The device palette (16)

Each entry: **what** it is · *affords when* · a short illustrative example.

1. **White caption box** — a hard-edged solid rectangle (white, sometimes black) holding the title over a full-bleed photo, corner-anchored. The default image-led slide. · *Affords when:* almost always in Keynote mode — this is the default slide. · A three-word claim set in a white box over a full-bleed photograph.

2. **Go-to-black breath slide** — a pure-black slide, or one white line on black, used as a rest between dense runs and immediately before a reveal. · *Affords when:* the pace has been busy and the next beat needs weight. · A pure-black slide holding a single line just before the turn.

3. **Giant-number shock** — one oversized figure dominating the slide, often over a busy image. · *Affords when:* the source contains a single striking statistic. · A single large figure (a dollar total, a count) filling the frame.

4. **Reused-photo run** — hold one image fixed across several slides while only the caption advances. · *Affords when:* one scene can host a multi-step point without a new image per step. · The same photo held across three consecutive consequence captions.

5. **Repeated-headline photo essay** *(body engine A)* — hold one title constant and swap the image across 6–9 slides, developing a single idea from many angles. · *Affords when:* one claim is best proven by accumulation, not enumeration. · One claim held constant while nine different images argue it from different angles.

6. **Two-beat tension pair** *(body engine B)* — force A on one slide, force B on the next; the concept is the paradox *between* them, never stated outright. · *Affords when:* the idea is a tension or contradiction. · "Unlimited capability" on one slide → "unmet basic need" on the next; the paradox lives between them.

7. **One-word turn** — a long visual setup collapses onto a single word. · *Affords when:* an accumulation needs a hard punctuation mark. · A montage of chaos resolving onto a single calm word; a run of dread resolving onto a two-word confirmation.

8. **Wordless slide** — a full-bleed image with no caption, spoken over. · *Affords when:* the image is self-evident or purely emotional and words would flatten it. · A silent closing image the presenter simply talks over.

9. **Recurring visual motif / diagram** — a single spine object reused as a build across the deck. · *Affords when:* the argument has a repeatable structure or a returning frame. · A pyramid filled tier by tier; a 2×2 matrix re-shown to locate each scenario; a divider whose active section is highlighted.

10. **Chained-caption climax** — captions that grammatically complete across consecutive slides ("…which will lead to… / …but at a cost…"), so clicking through *is* reading a sentence aloud. **This is the one place the strict spoken-prose / titles-only test applies** — reserve it for a climax run, not the whole deck. · *Affords when:* a causal cascade or scenario needs to feel inevitable. · A ten-slide forecast run whose captions complete one long sentence.

11. **Bookend** — open and close on the same image family. · *Affords when:* the deck has a clear first image worth returning to. · Open on a rainy street, close on a flood; open and close on the same wide landscape.

12. **Coined vocabulary** *(signature flavor — opt-in)* — invent 3–6 sticky terms as the deck's takeaway. · *Affords when:* the source introduces genuinely new concepts that deserve names; a thought-leadership register. **Do not invent words for content with no new idea.** · A handful of coined terms that name the deck's new concepts and become its takeaway.

13. **Borrowed-authority quote stack** *(signature flavor — opt-in)* — sequence quotes to build credibility (a thinker, then a local leader). · *Affords when:* the argument benefits from external validation and real quotes exist. · A short sequence of real quotes escalating from a theorist to a decision-maker.

14. **Anaphora section engine** — repeat a stem across a run to structure a section. · *Affords when:* several parallel proof points share a frame. · The same opening stem ("…brought to you by X") repeated across three slides.

15. **Humor as release valve** — one comic image at the pivot to disarm a skeptical room. · *Affords when:* the audience is tense or the turn is a hard sell. · A single comic image at the hardest-sell moment to break tension.

16. **Speaker-aphorism close** — end on the presenter's own line, not "Thank you." · *Affords when:* there is a single sentence that distills the whole talk. · A one-sentence maxim the presenter delivers as the final slide.

## Two recurring failure modes to avoid

- **Manufactured drama.** Fear runs, coined words, and authority quotes are powerful *when earned*. On flat or technical content they read as parody. When the source affords few devices, keep the base layouts and let the images do quiet work.
- **Caption-box residue.** Pasting a white caption box over a photo that already carries text can leave the old text bleeding out from under the box. When rendering, ensure each caption box fully covers any prior text — one clean box per slide.

## The six narrative spines (cross-reference)

Chosen in Stage 3, independent of mode. Minto (Spine 0) is the Boardroom default and stays available in Keynote. See SKILL.md → "Spine menu" for the full description; the short form:

| Spine | Shape | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · Minto / McKinsey | Answer-first, grouped support, action-titles read top-to-bottom | Boardroom default; also an image-led 2×2 or pyramid deck (Minto-with-photos) |
| 1 · Emotional arc | Dread → Turn → Reframe → Hope → Answer → Exhale | A talk that should move the room, not just inform it |
| 2 · Reveal / misdirection | Long setup recontextualized at a hinge | A point that lands hardest when the audience is first led the other way |
| 3 · Framework build | A motif/diagram assembled across the deck | An argument with a repeatable structure (a pyramid, a 2×2, "four lessons") |
| 4 · Forecast cascade → implication | Chained consequences → "what this means for *you*" | A causal chain ending in a direct implication for the audience |
| 5 · Teaching / method | Problem → concept → how-to steps → proof *(most content-neutral; default fallback)* | A how-to or method talk; the safe default when nothing else fits |
| 6 · Scenario-parallel | Name N futures, walk each as a mini-arc, land on synthesis | Several parallel futures or options compared, then synthesized |

Spines compose — a Framework build can open with an Emotional dread run. In Keynote mode the thesis may land late (slide 10–19). Boardroom + Minto keeps answer-first.

## Art-direction principle (Stage 3.5)

Keynote images are chosen as **metaphor, not illustration**. The image stands for the abstraction; it does not depict the words literally.

- fire / burning ice → a paradox of opposing forces
- a broken foot beside a running shoe → moving fast yet standing still
- a marble statue of a mythic hero → a fatal flaw inside a great strength
- a capsized ship with passengers still relaxing on deck → mistaking crisis for "normal"

When you hand the deck to the **art-direct skill**, ask for the *metaphor* behind each beat, then the photography/style direction and an AI-image prompt. Literal stock illustration of the caption is the thing to avoid.
